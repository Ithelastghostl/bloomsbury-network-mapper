Seed Intelligence Pipeline

How BFF discovers new potential donors through the networks of people it already knows

The Seed Intelligence Pipeline starts from BFF's existing contacts — supporters, friends, and known donors (your "seeds") — and maps outward through their professional networks to discover who they can introduce you to for new donations. Every recommendation answers the question: "Which of our people knows someone worth approaching, and how warm is that path?"

The system takes your spreadsheets of known contacts, researches each person's public profile, maps who they sit on boards with, co-invest with, and co-chair charities alongside, then scores every discovered introduction route by value and reliability.

Learnings, Successes & Improvements

What we learned from running 8 of your real contacts through the pipeline, what worked well, and what we'd improve to find more potential donors.

Successes

1. Name matching is conservative and correct

The trigram matcher correctly distinguished "Richard Couzens" from "Richard Cousins" (similarity 0.600, well below the 0.85 threshold), preventing a false merge with the late Compass Group CEO. Meanwhile, exact matches like "Lloyd Dorfman" and "Hayley Ronson" linked at 1.000. Zero false positives across 8 seeds.

2. Declared introductions score highest

Seeds with "introduced by" data consistently produce the best routes. James Reed (via Avi Goldberg), Hayley Ronson (via Jeff Shear), and Piers Clanford (via Rob Perrins) all scored confidence ≥ 0.907. The warmth tier 1 introability (0.95) combined with relationship confidence (0.95) gives a significant scoring advantage. This validates the spreadsheet as the most valuable data source.

3. Corpus cross-reference finds hidden connections

5 of 8 seeds had corpus matches. Lloyd Dorfman's network was enriched by finding Sir Harvey McGrath in 2 charity filings. Gerald Ronson appeared in 4 filings, corroborating the Hayley Ronson family connection. The corpus adds evidence depth that purely online research cannot provide.

4. Cost-efficient at scale

8 seeds cost an estimated $2.16 (~$0.27/seed). Projected full-run cost for all 349 seeds: ~$94. This is well within the $100 default cost cap. The two-phase augmentation (research then network mapping) is more efficient than a single monolithic call.

5. Scoring formulas differentiate effectively

Priority scores ranged from 0.573 to 0.743 across seeds, showing meaningful differentiation. Lloyd Dorfman's rich signal set (rich list, foundation, 11 appointments) pushed his capacity and influence scores to the top of the batch. Piers Clanford's "sport" keyword uniquely boosted strategic_fit to 0.800. The formulas are sensitive to the right signals.

Learnings

1. Contacts without declared connections need more hops

Lance Uggla and Richard Couzens produced zero introduction routes. The pipeline only found P2 connections (sector peers) for them — not close enough to generate scored routes. For contacts without spreadsheet-declared paths, a second-hop expansion from P2 connections would unlock additional donors to be introduced to.

Affected contacts: Lance Uggla, Richard Couzens (25% of sample)

2. US/international contacts discover fewer potential donors

Michael Lynton's primarily US-based network (Snap Inc, Sony) yielded zero corpus matches because BFF's corpus is UK Charity Commission filings. His contacts are harder to corroborate. International contacts may need additional data sources (SEC filings, US nonprofit registries) to discover more introducible donors.

3. Affiliation data dramatically improves donor discovery

Contacts with listed affiliations (The Reed Foundation, Berkeley Foundation, This Day Foundation) produced richer networks of potential donors. Contacts without affiliations (Lance Uggla, Michael Lynton) had sparser results. The affiliation acts as a research anchor — knowing which boards your contact sits on helps the AI find their co-trustees and fellow directors (who are the potential donors).

4. Supporters as introducers is validated

Piers Clanford (supporter, tier "Important") scored 0.700 priority and 0.967 confidence. His "sport" keyword alignment and declared Rob Perrins introduction made him one of the strongest results. This confirms the design decision: supporters are both excludable as candidates and valuable as introducers. The exclude_as_candidate + available_as_introducer split is working correctly.

User Manual

Step-by-step guide to running the Seed Intelligence Pipeline.

Prerequisites

  • A Bloomsbury Football Foundation account with admin access
  • Spreadsheet files in XLSX format (HNW targets or MASTER supporters)
  • An Anthropic API key configured in the system settings (for augmentation phases)

1. Importing a spreadsheet

1

Upload the XLSX file

Navigate to Seed Imports in the admin panel. Click New Import and upload your spreadsheet. The system auto-detects whether it's an HNW targets file or MASTER supporters file based on column layout.

2

Review parsed rows

After upload, the system shows a preview of parsed rows. Check that names, affiliations, and "introduced by" fields parsed correctly. Common issues: special characters in names, missing required fields, Salesforce metadata in early rows (auto-skipped).

3

Process the import

Click Process Import to trigger bootstrap. This creates entity records, seed entries, known contacts (for supporters), and introduction routes from "introduced by" fields. Typical time: 5-15 seconds for 300 rows.

Spreadsheet formats supported:
HNW targets: Columns in order: Tier, First Name, Last Name, Affiliation, Introduced By, Email, Funder Type, Sub Type
MASTER supporters: Columns in order: First Name, Last Name, Affiliation, Introduced By, Email, Funder Type, Sub Type, Tier
Both formats skip Salesforce metadata rows (rows 1-11) automatically.

2. Running augmentation

4

Start augmentation

From the seed list, select seeds to augment and click Run Augmentation. Or trigger augmentation for all seeds via the pipeline run page. The system processes up to 5 seeds concurrently, with a configurable cost cap (default: $50).

5

Monitor progress

The pipeline dashboard shows real-time status for each seed: research phase, network mapping phase, and cost accrued. Each seed takes approximately 45-90 seconds to fully augment (research + network mapping).

Cost awareness: Each seed costs approximately $0.28 to augment. A full run of 349 seeds costs approximately $97. The system will pause if the cost cap is reached. You can adjust the cap per-run.

3. Reviewing staged entities

6

Review quarantined entities

Navigate to Staged Entities. This shows people discovered by network mapping that the system couldn't confidently match or reject. For each, you see the AI-proposed identity and the closest existing database match. Choose: Promote (new person), Merge (same as existing), or Reject (not relevant).

7

Understand match confidence

The confidence percentage indicates how similar the staged entity is to an existing record. Above 90%: the system auto-merged already. Between 75-89%: the system flagged it for your judgement (often a title difference like "Sir Peter" vs "Peter"). Below 75%: the system auto-promoted it as a new person.

4. Understanding results

8

Read candidate recommendations

After the pipeline completes, the Recommendations view shows scored introduction routes. Each row represents: "Supporter X can introduce Target Y via connection Z." Sorted by priority score within each seed.

9

Interpret the scores

Priority score (0–1): How valuable is this introduction? Combines warmth of the path, cause alignment, financial capacity, influence, and strategic fit.
Confidence score (0–1): How reliable is the evidence? Combines identity certainty, relationship evidence depth, number of independent sources, and data freshness.

10

Take action

For each recommendation, you can: Accept (move to active pipeline), Reject (not a good fit), or Defer (revisit later). Accepted recommendations feed into the existing dossier generation and export workflow.

6. Troubleshooting
