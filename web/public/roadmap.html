Affinity Scoring — BFF Alignment Index
Score each lead on how likely they are to care about BFF's mission
Combine philanthropy signals, sector overlap (sport, youth, education, inner-city), geography (London focus), and stated interests into a single 0–100 affinity score. A hedge fund manager who already funds a youth football programme in Hackney scores far higher than one who only gives to opera. This directly prioritises the outreach list.

4
Expand to 2nd-Degree Connections
Harvest connections-of-connections to find hidden bridges
Currently we map seeds → direct contacts only. Run a second pass: for the top 50 highest-value leads, pull their co-directorships and charity trusteeships. This reveals bridge nodes — people who connect two otherwise-separate clusters — and surfaces leads who are 2 hops from multiple seeds, dramatically increasing reachability.

Event & Social Proximity Mapping

Community Detection & Cluster Labels
Identify natural groups within the network
Run Louvain or Leiden community detection on the full graph to identify clusters (e.g. "Shore Capital cluster", "Ronson family orbit", "NatWest board network"). Label each cluster by its dominant affiliation. This tells BFF which communities they already have a foothold in, and which high-value clusters they lack any connection to.

Seed Effectiveness Ranking
Score each seed by how many high-value leads they can reach
Not all seeds are equal. Rank the 62 seeds by: (a) number of 100m+ leads within 2 hops, (b) diversity of clusters they bridge into, (c) strength of their connections. The top 10 seeds become BFF's priority relationship-maintenance targets — these are the people whose networks unlock the most fundraising potential.

News & Trigger Event Monitoring
Surface timely reasons to reach out
Set up rolling news monitoring for the top 100 leads. Flag events like: new business sale (liquidity event), appointment to a charity board (signals giving intent), honour or award (good-news moment), company IPO, or retirement. Each trigger generates a time-sensitive outreach prompt: "Tony Bloom's Brighton just qualified for Europe — he may be receptive to other football causes."

Spouse & Family Network Expansion
Map family connections that often drive philanthropy decisions
Many large donations are household decisions. For each lead, identify their spouse/partner (if publicly known) and map their charity involvements separately. If Gail Ronson sits on a children's charity board while Gerald manages the family fortune, the approach should reference her interests. We already have Katinka Gissing (Naess heir + Ocado spouse) — scale this across the dataset.

10
Betweenness Centrality — Find the Super-Connectors
Identify people who bridge otherwise disconnected groups
Compute betweenness centrality for every node. People with high betweenness are "super-connectors" — they bridge separate clusters. If a single person connects the Tetragon/Polygon finance cluster to the Ronson property cluster, getting them onside opens two worlds. These are high-leverage intro targets even if their personal wealth is modest.

Property & Postcode Enrichment
Geo-tag leads for London-proximity and neighbourhood clustering
Use Land Registry and Companies House registered addresses to geo-locate leads. Tag those within the Bloomsbury/Central London catchment as higher-priority (they're more likely to attend events). Cluster leads by postcode to find neighbourhood-based introduction opportunities — neighbours in Hampstead or Notting Hill often know each other socially.

Briefing Pack Auto-Generation
One-page dossier for every meeting
Before any outreach, auto-generate a PDF briefing: the lead's wealth profile, their philanthropy interests, their connection path to BFF, recent news about them, suggested talking points, and the specific ask. This turns the database into an actionable tool that anyone on the BFF team can use, not just the person who built the network map.

14
Peer Comparison & "People Like Them" Model
Find un-connected leads who match the profile of existing donors
Build a profile of BFF's ideal supporter from the seeds who are already engaged (wealth band, sector, philanthropy domain, geography). Then scan the wider enrichment data for people who match that profile but aren't yet in the network. This is lookalike modelling for philanthropy — finding the next Tony Bloom before you have a connection to them.
